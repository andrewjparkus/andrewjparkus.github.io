Against the closing line uRAPM vs the Vegas spread on 13,068 games, 2014–2026, at information parity

Every accuracy number on this site compares us to other public ratings. The market is the only forecaster with money on it, and it is the harder bar. Here it is.

The short version. The closing spread predicts game margin at 12.856 RMSE (9.996 MAE). Given the same information — told who is out tonight, and nothing else — uRAPM scores 13.035. The market is still ahead by +0.179 points of RMSE, and wins 12 of 13 seasons. But our number is not redundant: added to the close, it measurably improves it — see below.

The information ladder

A closing line knows tonight's inactives. Our production margin harness aggregates on the realized minutes of the game being predicted, which is strictly more than the market has. Neither is a fair fight, in opposite directions. So four rungs, same 13,068 games, same pooled scale fit — and the answer is rung 2.

Knowing who is out is worth +0.149 RMSE to us — 46% of the blind gap against the close. Knowing how the minutes then fell among the players who dressed is worth −0.011: nothing, or slightly negative. That is a result in its own right. The whole value of the minutes oracle our published harness enjoys is the availability, not the distribution.

The parity gap is not a quirk of one arm: uRAPM hybrid +0.179, uRAPM pure +0.214, epm +0.109, darko +0.222. EPM closes more of it than we do.

Do we know anything the close does not?

The right test is not whether we beat the line — it is whether our number carries information the line has already priced. Fit margin ~ close + ours on strictly prior seasons, predict the held-out one:

  • our coefficient averages +0.324 and is positive in 12 of 12 seasons;
  • pooled out-of-sample RMSE moves 12.933 → 12.908 (−0.0253), helping in 9/12;
  • season-block bootstrap on the paired squared-error difference: 95% CI [−1.17, −0.14] — excludes zero.

Independently, the best convex blend fit on prior seasons wants 70% market and 30% us (12.932 → 12.909). A market that had fully priced our rating would want 100%.

And on the 7,185 games where an opening line exists, the correlation between our disagreement with the open and the line's own subsequent move is +0.302: the line tends to move the way we already were. Open 12.939 → close 12.819; we sit at 12.998, nearer the open than the close.

What this is and is not

This is a baseline, not a betting product.

Market lines are never fed back into the ratings — that is a measured dead lever here (market-shrinkage-into-ratings cost +0.12 win MAE). Nothing on this page is priced against the vig: beating a spread's RMSE by a tenth of a point is not the same as beating it after juice, and no claim of profitability is made or implied.

"Who is out" is read off the box score, and that flatters us.

A true pre-game inactives list only exists in our archive for 2025-26, so across 2014–2026 availability is taken as "appeared in the game". That also absorbs DNP–coach's decision, a rotation call the market cannot fully know either. The inactives-aware rung is therefore, if anything, slightly over-informed — the bias points toward us, and the close still leads.

Books excluded, and why.

ESPN serves in-play feeds beside pre-game ones. Their "spread" scores RMSE around 4 against the final margin, versus 11.7–13.8 for every honest pre-game book, and one of them in a median produced a fake 7.6-RMSE "closing line" during this build. Excluded here: Caesars (New Jersey) - Live Odds, Caesars Sportsbook (New Jersey) - Live Odds, DraftKings - Live Odds, ESPN Bet - Live Odds. The guard is both a name filter and a measured accuracy floor, because either alone can be evaded; the full per-book audit ships in vegas_provider_audit.json.

Which line this is.

ESPN carries an explicit open/close block only from late 2023. Before that there is one number per book. Reading it as a closing line is licensed by measurement, not assumed: on the 3,155 games where both exist they agree exactly 99.3% of the time and their RMSEs differ by 0.0007. The open-vs-close section uses only the seasons that genuinely carry both.
