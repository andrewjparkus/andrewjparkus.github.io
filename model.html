The model six-factor RAPM, re-solved every game-day

A six-factor RAPM re-solved as of every game-day across the NBA, G-League and NCAA on one NBA-equivalent scale, every value with a confidence interval. Ratings are descriptive — what a player has already done — unless a page is labelled a forward projection.

The six factors

Three offensive, three defensive, each in points per 100 possessions, sign-aligned so positive is always good.

FactorWhat it measuresPositive means
oTS Scoring efficiency — true shooting, including free-throw value scores efficiently
oTOV Ball security protects the ball
oSC Second chances created — putbacks and kickouts off the offensive glass, not a rebound rate creates extra possessions
dTS Shot defense — making opponents shoot worse suppresses opponent efficiency
dTOV Forcing turnovers forces turnovers
dSC Second chances denied denies opponent extra shots

Net is exactly the sum of the six, never a separate solve. The decomposition follows Russell Thomas's additive RAPM, and the ridge solver core is his reference implementation.

Limits

  • The error bars measure today, not next year. A band says how tightly the data pins down what already happened; it is not a forecast.
  • Game margins sit near the noise floor. Almost no modelling change moves them, and they are scored on the minutes players actually logged — rating quality given usage, not a pre-game forecast.
  • Defense is the soft spot. The inputs can't see contested shots or rim protection, so the defensive factors travel worst.
  • Cross-league jumps stay uncertain. College and G-League translations are heavily attenuated, so draft bands are wide by construction.
  • Coverage gaps. No public play-by-play before 1996-97 (NBA) or 2005-06 (college), and NBA feeds record no steals or blocks from 1997 to 2013.

All values in points per 100 possessions on the NBA-equivalent scale. Measured ratings carry measurement error bars; cross-league values carry an extra, non-shrinkable translation uncertainty.
