The one-liner

Classic six-factor RAPM gives one number per player per season — six components (points per 100 possessions, summing to net) split across offense and defense. This model re-computes that decomposition as of every game-day, for three leagues at once (NBA, G-League, NCAA) on one NBA-equivalent scale, each value with a confidence interval.

So instead of "Jokic was +8 in 2025-26," you get his rating as a daily curve across his career and through the playoffs, beside a college freshman's curve on the same axis — each with error bars. On the DataBallR scale, net +5 is clear All-Star level; the league centers near 0, the very best sit around +7 to +8, and the negative tail near −7.

The sixth factor, renamed. What older boards called "oREB/dREB" is here second-chance value (oSC/dSC): points added after the offense's own miss — putbacks and kickouts off the offensive glass, and on defense the prevention of those second chances. It is not a rebound rate (Jokic grades dSC ≈ +2.0, Wembanyama ≈ +0.9).

The six factors

Three offensive, three defensive, each in points per 100 possessions on the NBA-equivalent scale. Offense and defense are sign-aligned so that positive is always good.

FactorWhat it measuresPositive means
oTS Offensive true-shooting value (EFG + FT value) scores efficiently
oTOV Offensive turnover value — ball security protects the ball
oSC Offensive second-chance value (putbacks/kickouts off the o-glass) creates extra possessions
dTS Defensive true-shooting — making opponents shoot worse suppresses opponent efficiency
dTOV Defensive turnover value — forcing turnovers forces turnovers
dSC Defensive second-chance — preventing opponent second chances denies opponent extra shots

net = oTS + oTOV + oSC + dTS + dTOV + dSC. Offense is the first three, defense the last three.

How a "daily re-solve" works

Each day's value is a full RAPM under a 5-year rolling-window time-decay regime: a time-decayed ridge over every possession in the trailing five years, recent games weighted far more via a 550-day half-life (a game's weight is 2^(−age/550)). "As of day D" just means feeding it games dated in (D−5yr, D] — there is no carried-over state, so every day stands alone and the build is causally clean (planting a future game moves the as-of estimate by exactly 0.0). The window is chiefly for speed and cross-league consistency; the 550-day half-life keeps the snapshot fresh (a game ~1.5 years old already weighs half; a 5-year-old game ~2%).

The ridge solves each of the eight shot/decomposition components with its own tuned regularization penalty (a per-component α on offense and defense — the stabilized "LOCK" configuration), plus a small α = 10 group of garbage-time nuisance columns, over a decay-weighted, whitened design. It is a maximum-a-posteriori estimate under a Gaussian prior: the pure series shrinks toward zero; the prior-informed series shrinks each factor toward an informative box+EPM prior inside the ridge (next section).

Scale, by the numbers. The NBA design alone is ~7.1M possession-rows × ~5,800 player-columns. With ~10,200 league-days × five ridge solves each, that's on the order of ~51,000 solves, each over a million-plus possessions — embarrassingly parallel, since every day stands alone.
Net is the sum of the six. The headline net is exactly oTS + oTOV + oSC + dTS + dTOV + dSC (net := sum6) — there is no separate net solve to reconcile. The six factors come from four ridge leaf-metrics; offense is the first three, defense the last three.

Three views of the same day

The Explorer's Box-prior toggle switches every chart between three daily series. They share dates, leagues, and error bars; they differ only in what each factor shrinks toward.

ModeWhat it isShrinks toward
Pure The canonical, prior-free solve — textbook regularized RAPM. The reference calibrated to the public DataBallR ridge. zero, all six factors
Prior-informed (default) Same solve, but every factor is shrunk toward an informative box+EPM prior inside the ridge, strongest when lineup data is thin — so a rookie or fresh call-up starts somewhere sensible. box+EPM prior, all six factors
Box prior The prior itself, on its own: a daily curve with a band, showing the box-score skill expected before any lineup data. — (it is the target)

The shrinkage happens inside the ridge — a MAP estimate where each factor's coefficient is pulled toward a prior mean μ rather than toward zero (β ~ N(μ, ·)). Its strength is the ridge penalty itself: strong when a player's decay-weighted exposure is thin (rookies, call-ups, early season) and fading to near-zero as possessions pile up. So prior-informed and pure stay close for established players and on the headline net; the prior mainly rescues thin-data players. This replaced an earlier post-hoc blend — moving the prior into the solve is the single biggest accuracy gain in the current build (see accuracy).

What the prior is made of. Each factor's prior mean comes from a hyper-sparse, frozen linear map of predictive box + EPM rates (Dunks & Threes' forward-looking box model) onto that component — three-point value from 3P% and 3PA rate, turnover value from steal rate, second-chance from offensive-rebound rate, and so on across the eight decomposition components. A component earns a prior only if it improves the summed, out-of-time prediction on held-out cut dates — a sum-level gate, not in-sample fit. That discipline is what keeps per-factor gains from cancelling at the team level (a real trap: tuning each factor in isolation measurably hurt the net). The shipped stack priors all eight components on the sides where the map clears the gate.

The interesting part: what transfers between leagues?

We don't assume college production means the same thing as NBA production — we measure it, from two-league players (786 G-League → NBA, 1,252 college → NBA). For each factor we fit an affine translation: the slope is how much transfers, the correlation R how reliably.

Factor NCAA→NBA slopeR G-League→NBA slopeR

The model's whole personality is in this table:

  • Every slope is well below 1. Dominating a lower league barely moves your NBA number — a college player who is +5 in true shooting projects to roughly average NBA shooting.
  • Shooting transfers weakly (G-League R ≈ 0.1): efficiency against lower-league defenses says little about scoring on NBA ones.
  • Hustle / instinct carries over best — forcing turnovers (G-League R 0.47) and offensive second chances (R 0.34) reflect motor more than skill-against-weaker-competition.
Worked translation. A G-League player grading +1.5 on forcing turnovers (dTOV, the most-transferable factor, slope 0.77) projects to ≈ +1.2 on the NBA scale — but with a wide, non-shrinkable band. The same player at +1.5 in shooting (oTS, slope 0.10) projects to only ≈ +0.15: lower-league scoring almost doesn't carry.

Each translation also carries an irreducible uncertainty floor (Var_translate): the league jump is genuinely uncertain, so cross-league error bars stay wide and never shrink to zero, however many college games a player has. That honesty is the point — it's why a future MVP's lone college season can sit near replacement level on the NBA axis and still be "consistent" with stardom, as you can see on the Draft board.

Did the 5-year window change these? Almost not at all. Re-fitting every translation under the new rolling window moved each slope by ≤ 0.003 (NCAA→NBA oTS 0.241→0.242, dTS 0.263→0.260, dTOV 0.126→0.126). The window evicts a couple dozen long-retired two-league players from the fit (1,276→1,252 NCAA movers), but the transfer relationships are robust — the set of players who actually crossed leagues is unchanged, so the affine barely shifts. The cross-league translation is essentially identical before and after the regime change.

Confidence intervals

Every factor on every day carries a standard error, se = 100·√σ̂² / √Weff, where Weff is the decay-weighted possession exposure that day. The band widens with few effective possessions (a rookie's first weeks, a cup of coffee, a recent call-up) and narrows as minutes accumulate: at high exposure the net band runs ±1.5 or so; on a thin sample it balloons past ±5. Translated (college / G-League) factors add the non-shrinkable Var_translate floor in quadrature, so their bands stay permanently wide — the league jump is uncertain in a way more games can't fix.

The forward projection — 1 to 5 years out

Everything above is descriptive — already-measured RAPM. The 2026-27 page and the Explorer's + Projection toggle add the other half: a genuine forward model for "where is this player headed?" It clears the carry-forward bar above, and now runs one to five seasons ahead.

For each of the six factors × each horizon (1yr … 5yr) a separate gradient-boosted model (XGBoost) is trained on 30 seasons of history — from a player's recent trajectory (current value, lags, three-year slope), the box-prior's second opinion, the predictive box stats, age, and role/minutes. Three choices keep it honest:

  • Time-ordered validation — each model trains on the past and is scored strictly on the future, never on data it has seen.
  • A monotonic constraint — a higher current value can never lower the projection, ruling out perverse "great now, so worse later" swings.
  • Survivorship-honest decline — players who leave the league aren't dropped from the target; their factor fades toward replacement level, so the model learns realistic decline and exit instead of assuming everyone ages like the veterans who happened to stick around.

It beats plain carry-forward on every factor at every horizon, and the age-curve baseline on all six factors one year out (dTOV, for instance, at CV-MAE 0.096 vs 0.107). The one-year gain is real if modest; the 2-to-5-year horizons are net-new — the old model could only step a single season forward. net is the sum of the six projected factors (never modeled directly), and every projected point carries a band that widens with the horizon: five years out is genuinely more uncertain than one.

See it on the Explorer. Open any current player, flip + Projection on the Net chart, and the blue measured curve grows an orange dashed trajectory out five seasons, a confidence bar at each year. Jokic, for one, is projected to drift from +8 today toward the mid-5s by 2031 as the age curve bites — the honest arc of a great player past his peak.
Minutes feed it, but don't inflate it. A companion model projects each player's minutes per game (carry-forward + mean-reversion + an age curve) and allocates each team's 240 minutes across its roster. Those minutes drive availability and team win totals on the 2026-27 page — not the per-100 rating itself, so no one is rewarded twice for simply playing more.

Trade grades — Expected Wins Added (EWA)

The Grades page scores every in-season trade by Expected Wins Added: the win-probability a moved player added for his new team above a replacement-level fill, over the games he actually played there. It is a descriptive grade of how the move played out — not a long-term verdict.

PropertyEWA
Unit Wins. Small numbers — a moved bench player is worth fractions of a win over a partial season.
Baseline A replacement-level fill (repl_net ≈ −1.4 pts/100), not zero — so an exactly-replacement player grades ≈ 0.
Sign + to the acquirer, − to the sender. Zero-sum on a clean 1-for-1 swap (modulo schedule).
Window Only the games the player logged for the new team — retrospective, not a projection.

Each game's contribution is a win-probability shift. The player's net-above-replacement, scaled by his share of that night's minutes, becomes a margin nudge Δμ; EWA sums the resulting per-game Δp over the games he played:

Δp = Φ((m + Δμ)/σ) − Φ(m/σ),   Δμ = 5·(mᵢ/M)·(netᵢ − repl_net),   EWA = Σ Δp

where m is the team's actual margin that game, σ ≈ 14.3 is the game-model margin SD (same as the accuracy section), mᵢ/M the player's minute share, and netᵢ his daily net RAPM.

Worked example. CJ McCollum (WAS → ATL, 41 games) grades +0.78 wins: summed over those 41 games, his minutes-weighted edge over a replacement wing added about three-quarters of a win to Atlanta's expected total — credited +0.78 to ATL, −0.78 to Washington on that leg.

Using the site

One scale across measured and forward views. Measured pages report what was known as of a game-day; the 2026-27 pages are explicitly tagged as forward projections. Player names everywhere link into the Explorer.

Explorer →

Any player's daily six-factor curve with error bands across NCAA, G-League & NBA. The Box-prior toggle switches Pure / Prior-informed / Box prior. A college→NBA career reads as one continuous, color-coded line.

Teams →

Measured 2025-26 team RAPM, factor heat strips, roster contribution, WAR, and model-win context from the game model. This is historical team strength, separate from the forward 2026-27 projection.

2026-27 →

The explicitly forward-looking standings page: p10/p50/p90 wins, playoff odds, and team drilldowns with rating, GP, minutes, rookie, and fallback uncertainty flags.

Tables →

Ranked RAPM leaderboards — the full six-factor decomposition in NBA-equivalent pts/100, offense and defense sign-aligned so positive is always good. Click a column to sort by any single factor, not just net.

Trade →

Move players between two franchises; each team's expected win change (Δwins ± 80% band) updates live, with a six-factor attribution. A what-if on current ratings — not a contract or fit model.

Draft →

The at-draft translation of each prospect's college / G-League production onto the NBA scale, with 95% CIs. Bands are wide by construction, so most prospects cluster near replacement level — that wide band is the honest signal.

API export →

Versioned static exports for leaderboards, snapshots, and the public schema. Use the manifest for freshness, factor definitions, and file locations.
