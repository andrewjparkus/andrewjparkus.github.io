The metric leaderboard six impact metrics, two honest tasks

Our six-factor RAPM decomposition against Dunks & Threes' EPM, DARKO, Box Plus-Minus, RAPTOR and WAR — one normalized panel, leakage rules in the open, lower is better everywhere. No public site publishes this side-by-side.

What makes it honest. Every rating is used only as it was knowable — Task A walk-forward, Task B frozen at the prior season's end — and a model_vintage flag marks each source published live or retro re-fit, so a re-run never poses as a track record. Full rules in Methods.

EPM leads; our internal decomposition ranks last of five now that the BPM/RAPTOR coverage gap is closed — read the note below.

The coverage gap is closed — and the honest result is that BPM beats us. BPM and RAPTOR used to be scored partly on holes in a stale crosswalk: their panel held ~90% of league minutes through 2020 but only a fraction of the recent seasons. Expanding the bbref crosswalk (Q12; bbref join now 100%) lifts their post-2020 coverage to ~88%, on par with the id-native sources, so the eval now scores the metric rather than the gap. BPM (2.999) and RAPTOR (3.005) both beat our internal decomposition (3.134), which now ranks last of five. That it was an artifact is proven, not asserted: varying only the crosswalk moved BPM by exactly 0.000 in both parity-coverage windows (2015-2020 and 2003-2014) and lifted only the thin 2021-2026 window — an id join cannot repair a metric that was merely worse. At parity in 2015-2020, BPM (2.661) already beat us (2.764). The price of being right about coverage is that a free public box score out-retrodicts our RAPM.
A correction we owe you — RAPTOR's earlier number was not reproducible, and that was our mistake. RAPTOR reaches our panel as three differently-computed variants filed under one source label, all stamped with the same season-end date, and our scorer picked among them by whatever order the rows happened to sit in — so the figure we published before this build could swing between two genuinely different metrics across 18,556 player-seasons. A data-handling defect on our side, and the number was not reproducible. It is now pinned to one named variant — Neil Paine's estimated RAPTOR — and deterministic, with a fail-closed guard that makes the build refuse rather than silently re-roll. One caveat on reading the change: RAPTOR's value also moved with the crosswalk expansion that landed in the same build, so the shift from the old published figure is not attributable to this fix alone.

Methods & leakage rules

  • Nothing sees its own outcome. Task A ratings are as of the day before the game they predict (as-of ≤ D−1); Task B sources are frozen at season N's end and scored on season N+1, so the target season is never in the fit.
  • Apples-to-apples minutes. EPM and DARKO are scored with our pre-game recent-minute shares, not their own projections — the test isolates the rating from each vendor's minutes model.
  • Coverage is reported, not hidden. Missing players are replacement-filled, so thin coverage would score the gap, not the metric. Since the Q12 bbref-crosswalk expansion (join now 100%) BPM and RAPTOR hold ~90% of minutes through 2020 and ~88% by 2026, on par with Internal, EPM and DARKO (87–92% every season 2003-2026) — so BPM's win is not a coverage mirage.
  • RMSE within a window, corr across. Team-margin SD rises 4.44 → 4.97 → 5.24 across these eras, so cross-era RMSE is mechanically misleading. Corr is published beside every RMSE here.
  • DARKO, two ways. The daily re-score updates in-season (~96% player coverage); only pre-season-frozen DARKO exists on disk for older seasons — staler, not worse by design.
  • No blend ships. The internal+EPM blend is shown once, as an informative benchmark. Per an owner decision it is benchmark-only — not a product — no EPM-RAPM blend ships.
