The metric leaderboard six impact metrics, two honest tasks

Six public and internal player-impact metrics — our RAPM decomposition, Dunks & Threes' EPM, DARKO, Box Plus-Minus, RAPTOR and WAR — scored on the same two out-of-sample tasks, on one normalized panel, with the leakage rules stated in the open. Lower is better in every table. No public site publishes this side-by-side; this page is it.

What makes it honest. Every rating is used only as it was knowable at the time: game predictions are walk-forward (a rating as of the day before tip, never after), and the season task freezes each source at the prior season's end and asks it to predict a team's next-season margin. Each source also carries a model_vintage flag — whether the number is the metric as it was published live, or a current-model retro re-fit over as-of data — so a re-run never masquerades as a live track record. Where a source's panel thins, its coverage is printed next to its result rather than left to flatter the rest of the table: two of the six carry a coverage-limited tag, and the reason they trail is spelled out rather than banked. The internal+EPM blend appears once, labeled benchmark-only — not a product: no EPM-RAPM blend ships as a product.

Task A — predicting game margins (walk-forward)

Feed each source's team rating into the shared game model and score the predicted margin against the actual result, out-of-sample, over every game of 2024–26 (n = 3,242 games). RMSE is in points; each rating is causal — as of the day before the game, so no game informs its own prediction. DARKO appears twice: a fresh daily re-score that updates within the season, and the frozen pre-season snapshot (the only DARKO on disk for older seasons), which is necessarily staler.

Task B — retrodicting next season's team margin

The projection test, and the row where the two publicly-famous box metrics join. Freeze each source at a season's end, roll the roster forward with minutes-continuity weighting (the Snarr 2020 convention), and predict each team's next-season net margin. Pooled over 360 team-seasons, 2015–2026; strictly out-of-sample — the next season is never in the fit. RMSE is points of team margin; corr is with realized margin; scale b is the fitted shrink slope.

The bottom two rows come with a caveat that changes how you should read them — it is the next paragraph, not a footnote.

BPM and RAPTOR are not "far behind" — read the coverage column first. Most of that gap is a coverage artifact, not a quality result: the BPM/RAPTOR panel holds ~90% of league minutes through 2020 and then decays to 32% by 2026, so across the back half of this window the test is scoring the holes in their panel — where missing players are replacement-filled — rather than the metric itself. Score them where their coverage is intact and the gap all but closes: in 2015-2020, BPM's corr (0.822) sits with EPM (0.829) and DARKO (0.834), and BPM's RMSE (2.664) beats our own internal decomposition's (2.768). Only in 2021-2026, as the panel thins, does BPM's corr crater to 0.593. That is the signature of a thinning panel, not of a degrading metric. Internal, EPM and DARKO hold 87–92% coverage in every season 2003-2026, so comparisons among those three are coverage-clean throughout; the two public box metrics deserve the asterisk this column gives them.

Methods & leakage rules

  • Task A is walk-forward. Each rating is taken as of the day before the game it predicts (as-of ≤ D−1); a game never informs its own prediction. Pooled over 3,242 regular-season games, 2024–26.
  • Task B is out-of-sample by construction. A source is frozen at season N's end and scored on season N+1's realized team margin; the target season is never in the fit. Rosters roll forward with Snarr-2020 minutes-continuity weighting. Pooled over 360 team-seasons, 2015–2026.
  • Apples-to-apples minutes. EPM and DARKO are scored with our pre-game recent-minute shares, not their own projected minutes, so the comparison isolates the rating from each vendor's minutes model.
  • Coverage is reported, not hidden. A metric whose panel is missing players is replacement-filled, and the test then scores the gap instead of the metric. BPM and RAPTOR fall from ~90% of minutes through 2020 to 32% by 2026, so both carry a coverage-limited tag and their headline deficit is explicitly not presented as a quality verdict. Internal, EPM and DARKO hold 87–92% in every season 2003-2026.
  • RMSE within a window, corr across. Realized team-margin SD rises 4.44 → 4.96 → 5.22 across these eras, so cross-era RMSE comparisons are mechanically misleading. Corr is the scale-free read and is published beside every RMSE here.
  • Vintage honesty. EPM (date-backfill), DARKO (history pull) and the internal board are current-model retro re-fits over as-of data — flagged as such, never sold as a live track record. BPM, RAPTOR and WAR are the metrics as published.
  • DARKO, two ways. The daily re-score updates within the season (~96% player coverage); only pre-season-frozen DARKO exists on disk for older seasons, and it is reported separately because it is staler, not because it is worse by design.
  • No blend ships. The internal+EPM blend is shown once, as an informative benchmark only. Per an owner decision, benchmark-only — not a product — no EPM-RAPM blend is a product.
